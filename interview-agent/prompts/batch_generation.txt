=== HOW TO INTERPRET THE TARGET REQUIREMENTS ===

Before writing the questions, classify what kind of requirement each one is and apply the matching strategy:

A. TECHNICAL SKILL or TOOL (e.g. "Python", "Kubernetes", "REST API design")
   → Ask about a real-world scenario where this skill was applied, the tradeoffs faced,
     and what the candidate would do differently now.

B. BEHAVIOURAL or SOFT SKILL (e.g. "cross-functional collaboration", "stakeholder communication")
   → Ask for a specific past situation (STAR-style) that reveals the behaviour.

C. RESPONSIBILITY or OWNERSHIP AREA (e.g. "Lead API migrations", "Mentor junior engineers")
   → Ask how the candidate has owned or driven work in this area end-to-end.

D. DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE (e.g. "distributed systems", "HIPAA compliance", "financial modelling")
   → Ask how the candidate has applied this knowledge to solve a concrete problem,
     including constraints and decisions made.

E. ANYTHING ELSE (e.g. degree requirements, certifications, experience thresholds)
   → Reframe: ask what the candidate has built, delivered, or learned in the relevant
     space, not about the credential itself.
   → NEVER ask "Can you describe your experience with [exact JD phrase]?" — that is
     circular, confusing, and useless.

=== QUESTION QUALITY RULES ===

1. Questions MUST be open-ended — never yes/no, never "Do you know X?"
2. Questions MUST be answerable from genuine experience — never assume credentials
3. Combined and resume_based questions MUST quote or closely paraphrase resume evidence
4. jd_based questions MUST use a plausible real-world scenario or challenge as framing
5. Answer hints MUST be specific and grounded in what a credible answer looks like
6. Answer hints MUST NOT suggest fabricating experience
7. target_skill MUST be a concise competency noun phrase (e.g. "Distributed tracing", "Cross-team influence")
8. The question text MUST NOT echo the requirement verbatim
9. category choices: Technical | Behavioral | Situational | Role-Fit
